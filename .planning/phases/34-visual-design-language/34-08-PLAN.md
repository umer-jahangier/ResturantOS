<objective>
Close the phase: write down what was built, measure what was promised, and prove nothing regressed.

Three of the six definition-of-done items are documentation and measurement rather than
implementation, and they are the ones most often written as prose and never checked. The
vocabulary must be documented to the standard phase 20 set — every value tokenised and every
number re-derivable from the shipped stylesheet — because that is the standard that made phase 20's
contract survive a year of edits.

The operational-zone promise is the one this phase will be judged on. D-34-02's whole argument is
that the POS and the KDS stayed fast while everything else got richer. That claim needs assertions
that fail identically on every machine, not a stopwatch reading from a laptop. So the gate is the
computed-style set — no compositing filter anywhere in the subtree, no transition on the cart
mutation path, no animation on the board — and the wall-clock latency is a recorded number with a
generous ceiling beside it, clearly labelled as the weaker evidence it is.

Purpose: definition-of-done items 1, 3 and 6, and the phase's own honesty about what it shipped.
Output: a measured spec, a developer guide, the operational proof, a bundle delta and a full gate
sweep.
</objective>
