<objective>
Make routing configurable by the person who decides it. Plan 28-05 built the per-branch route tables and
the resolver; this is the screen that writes to them.

Until now, assigning an item to a station has been a curl-only operation — the endpoint exists, it is
permission-guarded and it is tested, and nothing in the product calls it. That is the second half of the
same gap plan 28-06 closed for stations themselves, and it is what stands between a tenant and D-28-04:
an order cannot span two stations if nobody can tell the system which items belong to which.

Two pieces of copy do real work here.

**The choice is per branch.** The menu item is shared across a tenant's branches — it has no branch of
its own, which is exactly why the route tables exist. So the screen has to say that this choice applies
to the branch being managed, or a multi-branch owner will assume they have set it everywhere. Before
plan 28-05 they would have been right, and wrong in a way that quietly re-pointed the other branch.

**The category rule is the one people actually want.** No manager wants to set a destination on two
hundred drinks one at a time. The category-level panel is the primary affordance and the per-item field
is the exception for the dish that does not follow its category.

Purpose: plan 28-14 configures a spanning order through this screen in a browser.
Output: a station field on the menu item form, a category routing panel, and destinations visible in the
list.
</objective>
