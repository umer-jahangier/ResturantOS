<objective>
Make the kitchen ticket independent of the browser.

Research §8's comparison table has one column that it says should decide the architecture: works if
the tab is closed. Every browser-driven transport fails it, and the reason is operational rather
than theoretical — during a service tills get locked, tablets sleep and Chrome evicts background
tabs. Research §9.3 decision 4 draws the conclusion: kitchen tickets are server-triggered, and
routing belongs to the server that already knows station assignments, not to whichever tablet
happened to fire the order.

26-07 already writes the ticket as a durable row at the moment the order is fired. This plan gives
the agent a way to come and get it: a per-branch credential, a claim-and-acknowledge pair of
endpoints with a lease, and a poll loop in the agent.

**This is the plan that pushes Phase 26 past the roadmap's 18-day estimate.** It is included
because a kitchen printer that only works while a tablet is awake is precisely the
"structurally present, behaviourally absent" outcome the defect register catalogues twenty-six
times. If the phase must be cut, this is the plan to defer — and the consequence, stated plainly,
is that kitchen tickets drain through the browser bridge from 26-09 and stop when the last tab
closes.

Purpose: the scope fence puts the kitchen ticket in scope, and a kitchen ticket that needs a browser
is not a kitchen ticket.
Output: agent enrolment, a claim channel, the agent's poll loop, and the enrolment card.
</objective>
