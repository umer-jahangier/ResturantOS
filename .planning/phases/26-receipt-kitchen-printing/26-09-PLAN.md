<objective>
Wire the browser to the agent, and build the ladder that guarantees a cashier who presses Print
always ends up with paper in their hand.

Research §9.4 sets out four rungs: agent reachable and printer online means silent ESC/POS with a
cut and a drawer kick and nothing on screen; agent reachable but printer offline means the job
queues and a badge says so; agent unreachable or the browser's local-network permission denied
means fall back to the browser bill; and no printer configured at all means go straight to the
browser bill. D-26-01 adds the rule that makes this a product rather than a diagram: the fall back
happens **without an error**.

The failure this plan exists to prevent is specific and this codebase has shipped its cousins
repeatedly — a control that appears to work and produces nothing. A print button that silently
no-ops when the agent is down is the same defect as a Save button that writes to `localStorage` and
reports success.

Purpose: D-26-01's fallback requirement and definition-of-done item 6.
Output: the agent client, the ladder, the status badge, and a test that walks every rung.
</objective>

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| HTTPS page to loopback HTTP | The product's origin reaches a local daemon across a browser security boundary |
| the browser to the branch LAN | A LAN-bound agent is reachable from any page the cashier opens |
| the ladder to the cashier | A degraded state is communicated, or is not |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-26-09-A | Denial of Service | A print attempt blocking settlement | high | mitigate | Bounded probe and print timeouts, asserted with fake timers; the ladder returns within a known bound and the fallback is immediate |
| T-26-09-B | Repudiation | A silent no-op on press | critical | mitigate | The enumeration test loops the closed outcome union and asserts every rung ends in a send or an opened bill; an unhandled outcome is a compile error |
| T-26-09-C | Information Disclosure | Product credentials sent to a local daemon | high | mitigate | The client never attaches an authorization header, tenant header or cookie; a grep gate asserts it |
| T-26-09-D | Spoofing | A hostile process listening on the configured loopback port | medium | accept | Any process on the till can already read the browser's traffic and the operator chose the port. The agent's own shared secret covers the LAN case. Documented rather than mitigated |
| T-26-09-E | Tampering | Two bills for one sale | high | mitigate | Rung two informs and does not fall back; the queued job will print and opening the browser bill as well is explicitly forbidden |
| T-26-09-F | Repudiation | A permission denial reported as an absent installation | medium | mitigate | The denial is a distinct outcome with its own message; detection is defensive so an ambiguous signal degrades to unreachable rather than misreporting |
| T-26-09-G | Repudiation | A badge showing ready because its probe failed | high | mitigate | The failed-probe case is written first and asserted to render the unreachable state |
| T-26-09-SC | Tampering | npm/pip/cargo installs | high | mitigate | This plan adds no package to any manifest. If one becomes necessary, halt and require the package-legitimacy audit plus a blocking human checkpoint |
</threat_model>
