<objective>
Report the cost, and build the check that would have caught the defect the last reconciliation missed.

22b's D-8 is half of this: seven reports exist and none of them is a profit and loss, a trial balance,
a cost of sales or a margin, so cost is invisible even though the ledger holds it. Plan 32-07 landed
the data. This plan surfaces it.

The other half is the sentence in 22b worth designing around: **the general-ledger-to-sub-ledger tie
held while cost was a thousand times wrong**, because the over-depletion and its journal entry
mirrored each other faithfully. Stock valuation equalled account 1300 to the paisa the entire time.
That reconciliation proves the wiring and says nothing about the arithmetic, which is exactly how a
1000× error survived to be found by driving a plate through the product and noticing a 4,310 percent
food cost.

So this plan builds two checks that are not that one:

**An independent recomputation.** Take an ingredient's cost from a source that never passes through
the depletion arithmetic — the unit price on the goods receipt that brought it in, which purchasing
holds and finance booked to inventory — multiply by the quantity the recipe says the sold line needs,
and compare. Two paths to the same number, sharing no code. A mirrored error moves both sides of the
GL tie and only one side of this one.

**A plausibility band.** A food-cost percentage is a bounded quantity in a real restaurant. A value
outside the band is reported as an anomaly, with its inputs, for a human to look at. It is not
corrected, because the wrong number is the evidence.

Purpose: reconciliation proves wiring, not arithmetic, and the last one that passed was wrong.
Output: cost and margin reported, plus two checks that a mirrored error cannot pass.
</objective>
