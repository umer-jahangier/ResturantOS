<objective>
Make the whole of D-30-01 true, and close a branch-isolation hole this phase found on the way.

The device API today is three endpoints: register, list, and a delete that sets a flag. There is no
rename — there is not even a name column until 30-03. No branch reassignment. No way to re-enable
what you disabled. No token rotation, so a leaked credential can only be dealt with by deleting the
device and registering it again under a new serial it does not have. D-30-01 asks for seven
operations and roughly two and a half exist.

Underneath that is something the research did not look for and the context does not mention.
`EmployeeService` routes every read and write through the OPA policy, passing the *record's* branch,
and its class javadoc explains at length why: the policy demanded branch isolation on every action
and nothing ever asked it, so a manager at one branch could read and modify salaries at another.
`AttendanceDeviceService` and `QuarantineController` were never given the same treatment. They are
gated by role permission alone, and their repository lookups are tenant-scoped and branch-blind. So
a manager with the attendance-manage permission can today disable, delete or read the registration of
a terminal at a branch they have no relationship with, and can resolve another branch's quarantined
punches onto their own employees. The policy that would refuse all of it is the same policy that was
a dead letter for employees — still enforcing nothing, on this surface, right now.

Purpose: D-30-01 in full, and the isolation the rest of HR already has.
Output: a complete device lifecycle API, an administrator-facing queue API bounded by 30-07's
allowlist, and authorisation on every one of them.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| a manager at one branch to another branch's terminals | Tenant-scoped, branch-blind lookups reach every device in the tenant today |
| an administrator to a terminal's operating system | The queueing endpoint is the only path from a person to a device command |
| the rotation endpoint to a live credential | Rotation mints a secret and invalidates one, and takes a physical device offline |
