How Finance works

Every tab in this module, explained for someone who runs a restaurant rather than someone who keeps books. Each section answers the same four questions, and the boxed rules are the ones that most often come as a surprise — why cash needs an open till and card does not, why a closed month refuses a back-dated entry, what a discount really does to your sales figure, and why a few actions ask for a code.

Every boxed rule here is tied to a test in this codebase. If the product stopped behaving the way a rule describes, that test would fail before anyone read the wrong sentence.

Guide
Takings

What you took today, and whether the drawers agree.

WHAT IT IS
The end-of-day picture of your money: what you sold, what you gave away, what the tax came to, what the bills actually added up to, how it was paid, and what each till counted at the end of the shift.
WHEN YOU USE IT
Every evening, when the last table has gone and the cashier is counting the drawer. It is the first screen Finance opens on, because it is the one question you ask every single day.
WHAT A TYPICAL ENTRY LOOKS LIKE
A Thursday: 26 bills, Rs 33,390 of food at menu price, Rs 5,342.40 of tax, Rs 38,732.40 taken in total — Rs 10,068.80 of it on card across 8 payments and Rs 28,663.60 in cash across 18. One till counted Rs 43,567 against an expected Rs 6,836.05, so it shows as Over by Rs 36,730.95, on that till and nowhere else. A second till was still open at the time, so it shows as still open rather than as an empty drawer.
WHAT IT AFFECTS ELSEWHERE
Nothing on this screen is typed in — every figure is the sum of orders that were closed in POS, so it changes when a bill is settled, refunded or voided. Each of those individual events is listed on Transactions, and the entries they wrote in your books are on Journal Entries and General Ledger. If a figure here looks wrong, Transactions is where you go to find out which bill caused it.
RULES WORTH KNOWING BEFORE YOU HIT THEM

Where the takings screen cannot work a figure out, it tells you why instead of showing you a zero.

A zero on a cash-up screen is a statement: it says the drawer matched, or that nothing was given away today. If the system does not actually know, printing a zero is not a blank — it is a lie you will act on. So a figure the system cannot compute shows the reason in the place the number would have been. Comps are the standing example: a fully comped bill is recorded in the same column as a discount, so the two genuinely cannot be told apart, and the screen says exactly that rather than guessing a split.

Checked by 2 tests in this codebase. If the product stopped behaving this way, they would fail.

Each till is shown on its own, and a shortfall on one is never cancelled out by an overage on another.

If one drawer is Rs 900 short and another is Rs 900 over, that is two things to look into, not nothing. Added together they would come to zero and you would never hear about either. So the cash-up screen lists every till separately with its own expected amount, counted amount and difference, and there is no combined figure anywhere on it to hide behind.

Checked by 2 tests in this codebase. If the product stopped behaving this way, they would fail.

A till still open and a till closed without anyone counting the drawer are shown as two different things.

Both leave the difference blank, and they mean opposite things. A till still open is a shift in progress and there is nothing to reconcile yet — a normal Tuesday evening. A till closed with no count means the shift ended and nobody counted the money, so nobody knows whether it matched. Showing both as the same dash would make the second one invisible, which is precisely the situation you would want to be told about.

Checked by 2 tests in this codebase. If the product stopped behaving this way, they would fail.

Settling in cash needs an open till; settling by card does not.

Cash physically enters a drawer, so the system insists on knowing which drawer — that is what makes an end-of-shift count meaningful. A card payment never touches a drawer, so it needs no till. If a cashier tries to settle a bill in cash before opening a till, the payment is refused with NO_OPEN_TILL and nothing at all is recorded against the order; open the till and the same settlement goes through. Waiting staff are a separate matter — they are not permitted to take payments at all, so they are turned away earlier and for a different reason. Handing the bill to a cashier is the designed table-service flow, not a fault.

Checked by 2 tests in this codebase. If the product stopped behaving this way, they would fail.

A discount lowers what the customer pays, not what your books call sales: the full menu price is still credited to sales, and the discount is recorded separately against it.

If a Rs 800 dish goes out at Rs 680, the books credit the whole Rs 800 to Food Sales (4100) and put the Rs 120 on Discounts Given (4920), which sits against sales rather than inside it. You can therefore answer 'what did we give away this month?' — a question a netted figure cannot answer at all, because the discount has already vanished into a smaller sales number. It also keeps the entry balanced: netting sales AND recording the discount would count the same Rs 120 twice.

Checked by 2 tests in this codebase. If the product stopped behaving this way, they would fail.
